```svelte

  label="Input trim"        <!-- required; also the accessible name -->
  family="cream"            <!-- FunctionFamily: cream | amber | red | bluegrey | green | orange -->
```

## Exact techniques used to achieve the hardware appearance

Per spec §6, the goal is "physical object with light and shadow", so nothing here
is a flat fill. Every value below is a token reference except pure
black/white alpha used for shading.

**Knurled cylindrical base** — `repeating-conic-gradient(from 0deg, …)` alternating
`var(--al-material-recess)` for 0–5° with `var(--al-material-surface-raised)` for
5–20°. The 20° period yields **18 ridges** (spec allows 16–20), and a
conic gradient is what makes them converge on the centre like a real knurled
collar seen from above. A `::after` overlay adds
`radial-gradient(circle at 36% 30%, white 0.16 → transparent 42% → black 0.55)`
so the collar reads as a rounded cylinder rather than a striped wheel.

**Cap depth and directional lighting** — the cap is an inset element at 27% versus
the base's 18%, so it is visibly smaller and sits *on* the base, as the spec
describes. Its fill is
`radial-gradient(circle at 33% 27%, var(--cap-hi), var(--cap-base) 46%, var(--cap-lo))`
— light from the upper-left, exactly the spec's radial highlight, using the three
family tokens. Depth comes from stacked inset shadows: a bright top inner edge
(white 0.5), a dark lower-right inner shadow (black 0.4), and an outer contact
shadow (black 0.55) that separates cap from collar.

**Pointer** — an SVG line inside `<g transform="rotate(angle 60 60)">`, from 57 to
37 in a 120-unit viewBox, `stroke-width` 4, round cap, stroked in the family's
`ink` token so it contrasts against each cap colour individually.

**Tick ring** — generated in script, not hand-written: `Array.from({length: 11})`
mapped to angles across `START_ANGLE = -135` to `+135` (**270° of sweep**, centred
on 12 o'clock), with trigonometry placing each line at radius 41.5→45.5, and
41.5→47.5 for majors. Every fifth tick plus the two ends is drawn thicker and more
opaque, so the extremes read as scale ends.

**Extreme numerals only** — exactly two `<text>` elements, placed by the same
trigonometry at radius 56, `text-anchor` end/start so neither overflows the dial.
They use `paint-order: stroke` with a 3px panel-coloured stroke under the fill, so
a numeral stays legible wherever it overlaps the tick ring or panel edge. No
numerals appear at intermediate ticks, per spec.

**Fixed reference triangle** — a `<polygon>` at the top of the viewBox, drawn in
the same SVG but **outside** the rotating group, so it is printed on the panel and
does not move.

**Engraved label** — `var(--al-font-label)`, bold, uppercase, with
`letter-spacing: var(--al-letter-spacing-engraved)` and
`text-shadow: 0 1px 0 rgba(0,0,0,0.85)`: the light fill plus hard dark shadow
below is what makes lettering read as cut into a dark panel rather than printed on
it.

**Controls on material, not on the page** — the fixture puts each knob on
`var(--al-material-panel)` with a `linear-gradient` sheen and bevel insets. A
cream cap or an engraved label judged against the playground's light grey page
would be judged against a surface it never appears on.

**Disabled** — `filter: grayscale(1) brightness(0.72) contrast(0.85)` on base and
cap, pointer and scale dropped to 0.45 opacity, and the dial removed from the tab
order (`tabindex="-1"`).

**Focus** — a 2px off-white ring with dark gaps, drawn **on the knurled collar**
inside the tick ring (decision D1.2.1), because an outer halo would cut through
the printed extreme numerals.

# Refinement pass — vintage visual quality

**Status: COMPLETE — awaiting explicit owner review of the running app.**

## What was wrong with the first pass

Judged at the actual display size rather than from the code, four things were
false about it:

1. **The cap was a plastic button, not a machined part.** One radial gradient
   centred hard up-left, with a bright inset highlight and a heavy dark crescent,
   reads as a glossy UI control.
2. **The knurl was a pattern, not a surface.** A repeating conic gradient lights
   every ridge identically, so at 88px it read as a striped or toothed ring.
3. **The scale was decoration.** Eleven marks over 270° are too coarse to be an
   instrument scale, and majors were barely distinguishable from minors.
4. **The knob and the panel were the same value.** The skirt's outer edge sank
   into the panel, so the control had no silhouette and the lighting on it looked
   like an effect rather than a consequence.

## The construction now

Five distinct physical parts, each with its own job, all lit by one source (key
light from the upper-left, azimuth 315° — D1.2.7):

| # | Part | How it is built |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Contact shadow** | Three stacked CSS box-shadows on the skirt element: 1px/1.5px at 0.75, then 2px/4px, then 5px/9px and 9px/16px at falling opacity. Tight and dark at the contact, wide and soft beyond it. |
| 2 | **Knurled skirt** | 18 annular sectors generated in script. Each is split into a lit face and a cut face; each face's opacity is computed from `cos(mid − 315°)`, so ridges turn into and out of the light. Cylindrical falloff over the top, a dark machined edge stroke, and a skirt base one step lighter than the panel so the part separates. |
| 3 | **Machined face** | An annulus between the cap and the skirt: dark plastic with the same key light at much lower albedo, plus a lit arc on the upper-left edge to read as a turned chamfer. |
| 4 | **Groove** | A radial occlusion gradient whose stops are computed from `RIM_R`/`FACE_R`, plus a hairline dark ring. This is what makes the cap sit *proud* of the face rather than painted on it. |
| 5 | **Cap dome** | Family `hi → base → lo` radial ramp (bright stop held only to 13%, base by 38%, shadow tone from 72%), a lower-right terminator gradient, an elliptical specular at 26% white, a weak bounce sheen low-right, a warm veil and a neutral scrim, then a clipped inlaid pointer. |

**Pointer** is three passes inside a `clipPath` on the cap, so it can never
overhang: a 3.1-wide dark channel offset down (the cut), a 1.9-wide line in the
family's `ink` token (the paint/inlay), and a 0.7-wide light edge offset 0.75px
(the lit lip of the inlay). It is generated with the value's angle and rotates
inside the clip, so the lighting never moves with it.

**Bevel between cap and face**: two arcs at `RIM_R − 0.45`, one light on the
upper-left quadrant, one dark on the lower-right, plus a hairline cap edge. The
cap also carries `drop-shadow(0 0.8px 0.9px)` so it is separated from the face by
light rather than by an outline.

## Working method (and a mistake worth recording)

Every judgement in this pass was made on the running app at 88px, not on the
code. During the pass I also captured enlarged views for diagnosis — and for
several iterations I was unknowingly inspecting a **3× body zoom** left over from
an earlier diagnostic, which made the knurl look like a gear and the caps look
blown out. The corrections made in that window were wrong-headed. After finding
it (by printing `getComputedStyle(document.body).zoom`), I reset the zoom, added
the check to the capture routine, and re-judged everything at true size and at
2× (which is what a retina display actually shows).

## Unresolved limitations

1. **Saturation of green and red is set by the approved palette.** The brief asks
   for "deep, muted vintage green" and "restrained vermilion". The uniform warm
   veil and neutral scrim take the edge off both, but the base hues
   (`#4d9461`, `#bd4637`) are Task 1.1 tokens, and D1.1.1 makes them
   authoritative. Changing them is a palette decision for you, not a knob
   decision — say the word and it is a three-token change.
2. **No framework tests.** Unchanged from the first pass: the project has none and
   this task may not add one, so `check:knob` guards the source contract and all
   behaviour above was measured in a real browser.
3. **The knurl is a suggestion at 88px.** 18 flutes over a ~3.5px band cannot be
   more than texture at that size; the facets are deliberately low-contrast so it
   reads as grip rather than teeth. It is more defined at 2×, which is what most
   displays actually render.
4. **`filter: drop-shadow` on the cap** is one of the few per-frame raster costs
   in the component. With 11 knobs on the page there is no measurable cost, but a
   page with hundreds would want it replaced by a drawn shadow.
5. **Still no vendored condensed label font** — the label uses the OS stack, as
   recorded in D1.1.5.
