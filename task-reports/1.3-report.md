## The cap — how the concave profile is made

### Geometry

```
cap        36 x 13 units, rx 4.5          2.77 : 1, rounded short ends
front wall the same shape, 2 units lower   only its bottom 2 units show
shadow     the same shape, black 55%,      offset (+1, +2.5): down and right,
           blurred 1.4                     never anywhere else
shaft      3 x 23, rx 1, nickel            5 units proud of the cap each side
```

The viewpoint is the knob's: top-down, raised just enough to see a sliver of
the object's front wall. The light is the knob's: one key light, top-left.

### The hollow gradient (vertical, back lip → front lip)

A concave trough lit from the top-left has a definite signature, and it is
the opposite of a dome's. The upper wall of the trough slopes *away* from the
light and is in shade; the lower wall slopes *toward* it and is lit. Each lip
is itself a small convex rim: the back lip's outer rounding faces the light
(brightest line on the cap); the front lip is bright on its inner slope and
dark on its outer rounding. That is what the stops encode:

| Offset | Token | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0% | `hi` 70% / `base` | back lip, outer rounding |
| 4–11% | **`hi`** | **back-lip crest — highlight band 1**, held |
| 15% | `base` 82% / `hi` | the lip's inner shoulder, a quick turn |
| 20% | `base` 48% / `lo` | upper wall, in shade |
| 29–44% | **`lo` 80% / black** | **the hollow — darkest, held** |
| 56% | `base` 42% / `lo` | the trough floor, lifting |
| 69% | `base` 84% / `lo` | lower wall, climbing |
| 79% | `base` 72% / `hi` | lower wall, lit |
| 84–91% | **`hi` 86% / `base`** | **front-lip crest — highlight band 2**, held |
| 95% | `base` 70% / `lo` | front lip, outer rounding |
| 100% | `lo` 80% / black | the edge turning down into the front wall |

Three overlays, all clipped to the cap and all neutral alphas (light, not
colour): a horizontal gradient that lights the left end (white 22%) and
shades the right (black 24%); an elliptical "pool" (black 20% → 0) that
darkens the hollow toward the middle of its length, so the trough reads as
a shallow dish open toward the ends; and a small specular (white 38% → 0)
where the back lip is nearest the light. A 0.5 px hairline in `lo`/black at
45% outlines the shape so the rounded ends read against the panel.

The only colours are the cream tokens, `--al-function-cream-{hi,base,lo}`,
read through `functionToken("cream", …)`. `check:fader` fails on any hex
literal that is not neutral black or white.

## The slot, the shaft, the print

**Slot.** Three stacked rounded rects and one clipped filter:

1. `--al-bevel-light` at 55%, the slot shape grown 1 unit and pushed 1.5 down
   — shows only as the lit lower/right lip;
2. `--al-bevel-shadow`, the same shape pushed 1 up — the dark upper/left lip;
3. the floor, `--al-material-recess`, a hair lighter over its last 8% where
   the far end wall catches light;
4. inside a clip of the slot shape, a 3-unit black ring on the slot's edge at
   80%, Gaussian-blurred 1.2 and offset (+0.6, +1): a soft inner shadow,
   heaviest under the top-left lip, that cannot spill onto the panel.

**Shaft.** 3 units wide in a 7-unit slot; a horizontal gradient from
`--al-material-metal-shadow` through a 62%-white lift of `--al-material-metal`
and back, with a 0.5 black-55% outline. It is part of the carriage group with
the cap and shows 5 units beyond it at both ends.

**Print.** Majors are the law's own breakpoints inside `[min, max]`, plus the
two ends; each major carries a numeral; one minor sits halfway between
neighbours. Numerals are 7.8 px tabular mono in `--al-text-secondary` with a
2 px panel-coloured halo (`paint-order: stroke`), right-aligned 2 units clear
of the ticks. Bipolar scales print their sign (+10 … −60); unipolar ones do
not (100 … 0). An inner numeral that would land within 9 units of an end
label gives way to it — this is what stops "+5" from printing under a "+6"
ceiling. Unity, when the range contains it, is printed in `--al-text-primary`
and a little heavier, the knob's reference-dot convention. All of it lives in
`data-part="scale"`, drawn before and outside the carriage; `check:fader`
fails if `capCy` ever appears in that block.

Tick weights are the knob's: minors 0.72 px at 45%, majors 1.2 px at 90%,
both `--al-material-metal`.

## The law

```
CONSOLE_TAPER   −60 → 0     −40 → .13    −30 → .225   −20 → .345   −10 → .495
                 −5 → .61     0 → .73     +5 → .865   +10 → 1
```

`fractionOf(value)` and `valueAt(fraction)` both interpolate the same table,
re-normalised to the instance's `min`/`max`, so the cap sits where the printed
scale says and a drag lands on the value under the pointer. With `taper = null`
both are linear and majors fall every `majorEvery` units. The Send A fixture
(−40 … +6) and the Blend fixture (0 … 100 %, linear) show both paths.

Travel is **relative and one-to-one**: a cap grabbed under the pointer stays
under it; a press on the slot beside the cap moves the cap from where it is,
it does not jump — the way a physical fader behaves. Shift slows a drag to
0.2× and makes it fine (see D1.3.2).

### What changed

* `Fader.svelte`: `CAP_W 36 → 25`, `CAP_H 13 → 20`, `CAP_RX 4.5 → 5`. Nothing

  clearance derive from the constants and still hold (measured below).

  the shorter side. Still 59 assertions.
* `decisions-log.md`: D1.3.5 records the override and the round-2 shaft
  removal together, so the two spec deviations are findable from the log.
